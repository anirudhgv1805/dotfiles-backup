If you're using the latest Lua-based Hyprland config, the best practice is to split it into small modules instead of putting everything in one hyprland.lua.

A common structure is:

~/.config/hypr/
├── hyprland.lua
├── config/
│   ├── variables.lua
│   ├── monitors.lua
│   ├── binds.lua
│   ├── input.lua
│   ├── decoration.lua
│   ├── animations.lua
│   ├── general.lua
│   ├── rules.lua
│   ├── autostart.luac
│   └── misc.lua
└── scripts/
    └── ...

Then hyprland.lua becomes your entry point:

require("config.variables")
require("config.monitors")
require("config.general")
require("config.input")
require("config.decoration")
require("config.animations")
require("config.rules")
require("config.binds")
require("config.autostart")
require("config.misc")

A typical responsibility split is:

variables.lua – modifier keys, app names, colors, reusable constants.
monitors.lua – monitor layout, resolution, scale, workspaces assigned to monitors.
general.lua – gaps, borders, layouts, focus behavior.
input.lua – keyboard, mouse, touchpad, repeat rate.
decoration.lua – rounding, blur, shadows, opacity.
animations.lua – animation settings and curves.
rules.lua – window rules, workspace rules, floating rules.
binds.lua – all keybindings, mouse bindings, submaps.
autostart.lua – programs started when Hyprland launches.
misc.lua – anything that doesn't fit elsewhere.

For example, variables.lua might look like:

local M = {}

M.mainMod = "SUPER"
M.terminal = "ghostty"
M.browser = "zen-browser"
M.fileManager = "thunar"

return M

And binds.lua:

local cfg = require("config.variables")

hl.bind(cfg.mainMod .. " + T", hl.dsp.exec_cmd(cfg.terminal))
hl.bind(cfg.mainMod .. " + B", hl.dsp.exec_cmd(cfg.browser))

This way you only define things like your terminal or modifier key once.

As your configuration grows (100+ binds, custom scripts, multiple monitors), this modular layout is much easier to maintain than a single large file. It's also close to how many experienced Hyprland users organize their configs, making it easier to borrow ideas from public dotfiles.
